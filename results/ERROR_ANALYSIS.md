# Test-set results and error analysis (Phase 5)

Generated by `python -m src.train --final && python -m src.evaluate`. The test set (6,782 rows,
fixed by seed 42) was scored once. Hyperparameters, thresholds and the choice of model were all
fixed on validation beforehand. Numbers are mean ± std over seeds 42, 43, 44.

## Headline (DEPLOYABLE, no `duration`)

| Model | Test PR-AUC | Test ROC-AUC | Brier | Recall @ t* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trivial baseline (prior) | 0.117 | 0.500 | 0.103 | 1.000 |
| Decision tree | 0.399 ± 0.003 | 0.755 ± 0.001 | 0.087 | 0.475 ± 0.012 |
| Random forest | 0.438 ± 0.005 | 0.796 ± 0.002 | 0.083 | 0.521 ± 0.011 |
| HGB, class-weighted | 0.441 ± 0.007 | 0.797 ± 0.001 | 0.149 | 0.520 ± 0.013 |
| HGB, unweighted (ablation) | 0.446 ± 0.003 | 0.801 ± 0.002 | 0.082 | 0.497 ± 0.020 |

- **Boosting does not meaningfully beat the forest.** The three ensembles are within about one
  standard deviation of each other on PR-AUC, ROC-AUC and the gains curve (F3, F5).
- **Class weighting does not help ranking but harms the probabilities.** The unweighted ablation scores
  slightly higher on PR-AUC, and the weighted model's Brier score nearly doubles (0.082 → 0.149).
  F9 shows why: weighting pushes every score up, so a predicted 0.6 means about a 22% chance of a
  subscription.
- **Validation was slightly optimistic.** Every model scores about 0.02 lower on test than on
  validation (e.g. HGB 0.460 → 0.441), which is expected after picking the best configuration on
  validation. The ranking of the models does not change.
- **The leak:** adding `duration` raises test PR-AUC by 0.15–0.18 for every model, 28–29% of the
  leaky score (F2b_duration_gap_test).

## Business view (chosen model: HGB class-weighted, highest validation PR-AUC)

- Calling the top 20% of clients finds 62% of subscribers (lift ≈ 3.1); the top 10% finds 42% (F5).
- At the validation-chosen threshold (seed 42) the model calls 14% of clients; 44% of those calls
  subscribe (precision) and it finds 52% of all subscribers (F7).
- Profit (call cost 5, reward 50): the threshold chosen on validation earns 17.6k on the test list,
  versus 5.7k for calling everyone. With reward 25, calling everyone loses money (−14.1k), but the
  model earns +5.7k (F8).
- The profit-maximising threshold for the weighted model sits far above cost/reward (0.44 vs 0.10).
  For the unweighted model it sits close to it (0.12). Calibrated probabilities can be used directly
  as a decision rule, and weighted ones cannot.

## What the model relies on (F6, permutation importance on test)

`poutcome` (0.14 drop in PR-AUC) and `month` (0.12) dominate, followed by `contact`, `day`, `pdays`
and `housing`. Client demographics (job, education, marital) matter very little.
`previously_contacted` shows zero importance because it is fully redundant with `pdays` (= −1):
shuffling one leaves the same information in the other, so permutation importance splits or
hides shared signal.

## Failure modes

1. **First-time contacts carry little signal.** 82% of test clients have `poutcome = unknown`.
   On this slice PR-AUC is 0.31 vs 0.75 for `poutcome = success`. 16 of the 20 most confident
   false negatives are first-time contacts, and none of the missed subscribers had a previous success.
2. **`contact = unknown` / May campaigns are nearly invisible.** The model calls under 1% of
   `contact = unknown` clients and finds only 8% of the 79 subscribers among them. In May (the busiest
   month, 7% positive rate) recall is 22%. 13 of the top-20 false negatives are May calls with unknown
   contact type. These rows come mostly from the early 2008 campaign, where little was recorded.
3. **Missed subscribers were persuaded during the call.** False negatives have a median call length
   of 656 s, against 161 s for true negatives (shown for context only; the model does not see it).
   Whatever turned them into subscribers happened on the call, and no pre-call feature records it.
   This is the leak from the other side.
4. **Over-trust in a previous success.** All 20 most confident false positives have
   `poutcome = success`. The model calls every such client (recall 1.00) and 62% subscribe, so this is
   still a good bet, but it explains the confident misses.

## Fairness note

The model calls 68% of clients aged 60+ and 51% of students, against about 9% of clients aged
40–59, because their base rates are much higher (32% and 28%). Ranking by age-correlated features
is accurate on this data but means some groups are contacted far more often. This should be
weighed before deployment (see the limitations section in the report).
